## Invention Summary

A collapsible bicycle helmet comprising:

1. **Honeycomb protective liner** made of overlapping thermoplastic cells
2. **Outer shell + inner honeycomb liner** (two-part construction)
3. **Quarter-turn twist actuation**: shell rotates ~90° relative to liner → cells lock into rigid expanded configuration
4. **Reverse twist**: cells collapse flat for compact storage
5. **Radial cell arrangement** around the head
6. **Impact absorption** through progressive cell wall crumpling

**Critical features:**

**What it does NOT disclose:**

## The Twist Actuation: Deep Analysis

### What I found in helmet patents:
- **US11857020**: Crown rotates **180°** relative to base body (not 90°), using a pivot + locking clip. Different mechanism — the crown flips over, not a twist-lock.
- **US11191316/US11589634**: Side chassis rotate relative to center chassis (not shell vs. liner).
- **WO2023233224A1**: End plates engage by manually stretching the structure (not twisting).
- **US9743704B2**: Panels hinge and snap-lock with bosses/catches (not twist).
- **CA2561540C**: Shell sections **slide** (not rotate) with pivot lock.
- **EP3391765A3**: Shell rotates relative to liner — but for **impact buffering**, not actuation.
- **CA2970532A1**: Shell rotates relative to head — for **impact decoupling**, not actuation.
- **GB2567411A**: Folding helmet with interlocking mating surfaces — uses hinge + fixings, not twist.

### What I found in general mechanical engineering:
- Quarter-turn twist-lock mechanisms are **extremely well-known**:
  - US4893978: "one-quarter turn twistlock fastener with very positive quarter turn latching positions"
  - US6575682: "quarter turn fastener... rotated through an arcuate extent of up to approximately 90°"
  - US9200660: "quarter turn fastener... rotated 90 degrees clockwise"
  - US12736079: "quarter turn retainer... rotated approximately 90 degrees"
  - US20110110716A1: "quarter turn locking mechanisms... bayonet catch"
  - US4653970: "quarter-turn fastener... spiral cam slot... 1/4 turn"
  - US5123795: "quarter turn fastener... one fourth of the way around the circumference"
  - Plus: bayonet couplings, camera lens mounts, twist-lock bottle caps, etc.

### What I did NOT find:
- A patent disclosing a **quarter-turn (90°) twist** of the shell relative to the liner to lock/unlock a honeycomb structure **in a helmet**.

### But:
- Quarter-turn twist-lock mechanisms are **ubiquitous in mechanical engineering** (dozens of patents, 1970s-2020s)
- Applying a known mechanical principle to a known structure is **obvious** under 35 U.S.C. § 103 (KSR v. Teleflex)
- The US11857020 patent already teaches rotation between helmet components as an actuation mechanism
- An examiner would likely combine US11857020 (helmet rotation) + US4893978/US6575682 (quarter-turn lock) → reject under § 103

## Honest Verdict

**Core concept is heavily anticipated.** The combination of:
- Honeycomb liner ✓
- Overlapping/staggered cells ✓
- Locks rigid when actuated ✓
- Collapses flat ✓
- Impact absorption by cell crush ✓

...is disclosed in **WO2023233224A1** and the **US12268268B2/US12740611** family. This is a crowded, commercially active field (Closca, FEND, Overade PLIXI, Newlane all sell folding helmets).

**What survives as potentially novel:** the specific **quarter-turn twist actuation** — shell rotating 90° relative to liner to switch rigid↔collapsed. No patent found disclosing this exact mechanism in a helmet. However:

1. It's a **narrow claim** at best
2. It's **likely obvious** in view of:
   - US11857020 (rotation between helmet components)
   - US4893978 / US6575682 / US9200660 (quarter-turn twist-lock fasteners — decades of prior art)
   - KSR v. Teleflex (applying known mechanical principle to known structure = obvious)
3. An examiner would likely reject it under § 103 as an obvious combination
4. The **specific geometry** of how the twist actuates the honeycomb cells (cam surfaces, detent positions, etc.) might be the only protectable aspect — but this would be a very narrow design patent or utility patent claim

**This is a directional read, not a patentability opinion.** For a real filing decision you need a classification-driven search (CPC **A42B 3/125**, **A42B 3/32**, **A42B 3/128**) by a registered patent attorney.

## Deep-Dive Update (v4): Is the Quarter-Turn Twist Actuation Novel?

Follow-up search (2026-10-09) targeting the only surviving differentiator — shell rotates ~90° relative to the liner to lock the honeycomb rigid. Five query framings were run through the Hermes `web_search` backend (bypasses the throttled device IP), plus direct extraction of the top candidates.

### New references found (not in v3)

| Ref | Title / Assignee | What it discloses | Impact on twist claim |
|-----|------------------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **US11944149B2** | Helmet — **MIPS AB** | Inner shell + outer shell + **sliding interface** between them; a **switch** selectively switchable between first/second discrete modes that allow or restrict **relative sliding** of the shells | Closest shell-to-shell *actuation* mechanism. Not a twist-lock and not a collapsing mechanism (it is rotational-impact / MIPS), but an examiner could cite it for "user-actuated relative movement between shell components" |
| **CA2944358A1 / EP3125712A1** | Locking liner for helmet | Liner lock between shell and liner | Liner locking concept — no twist |
| **US9554611B2** | Foldable protective helmet (Arrouart) | Foldable shell, commercial folding lineage | Background art |
| **WO2018052940A1 / US10959480B2 / US11678711** | Machine-vendible foldable bicycle helmet | Foldable honeycomb-matrix bicycle helmet family (antecedent of the US12268268/US12740611 "expandable" line) | Reinforces anticipation of elements 1–5 |
| **CA3096994A1** | Adjustable helmet assembly | Locking mechanism = **clamp / lever / cam** function; classified **A42B 3/32** | Shows cam/lever locking in the collapsible-helmet class |

### CPC class confirmed

- **A42B 3/32** — *Collapsible helmets; Helmets made of separable parts; Helmets with movable parts, e.g. adjustable*
- **A42B 3/328** — *. . with means to facilitate removal, e.g. …*

These are the correct primary classes for a full attorney-led search.

### US11857020 — re-read (the rotation reference)

Claim 1 confirms: an impact-resistant **base body** + an impact-resistant **crown**, **coupling means** allowing the crown to move **relative to the base body** between a protective position and a stowed position, with **releasable locking means** locking the two together in each position, and the description explicitly uses **rotation of the crown relative to the base body**. This is **rotation between two helmet components + releasable lock** — the exact functional genus of the twist claim, differing only in the *degree* of rotation (full rotation/stow vs. a 90° twist) and in the structure being a honeycomb liner.

### Verdict on the twist claim (unchanged, now better supported)

No patent was found that discloses a **quarter-turn (≈90°) twist of an outer shell relative to an inner honeycomb liner to switch the liner between a rigid expanded state and a flat collapsed state.**

However, the surviving claim is narrow and faces a strong § 103 (obviousness) posture:

1. **US11857020** already teaches *rotation between helmet components + releasable locking* as the collapse/stow actuation.
2. **US11944149 (MIPS)** teaches a *user-operable switch* that toggles the *locked / sliding* state between an inner and an outer shell.
3. **Quarter-turn / bayonet twist-locks** are ubiquitous mechanical art (US4893978, US6575682, US9200660, US5123795, US4653970 — already cited in v3).
4. Substituting a known quarter-turn fastener for the known rotation-and-lock of US11857020, applied to the known honeycomb-collapsing helmet of WO2023233224A1, is a textbook *KSR v. Teleflex* combination.

**Bottom line:** the twist actuation is the only element not literally read on by a single reference, but it is very likely obvious in view of the above. Utility-patent protection here is a long shot. The more defensible paths remain a **design patent** on the visual appearance and/or a **provisional** to hold a priority date while an attorney runs the A42B 3/32 classification search.
